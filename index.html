Build Your Six-Number Dashboard

Six numbers. One sheet. Reviewed weekly. Each one triggers a specific decision.
1

Your business — at a glance

The dashboard is built for your business at its current stage.
The one strategic priority — it sets your sixth number.
2

Define your six numbers

Three financial, three operating. For each: the specific metric, where it lives today, and its current value.
These six dimensions are fixed — the lesson is specific that this is what a owner dashboard measures. Your job is to name the specific metric inside each one, for your business. Pick the number that, if it moved, would change a decision.
3

Set the trigger for each number

A number on a dashboard does nothing. A number tied to a decision does the work.
For each metric you named: what movement triggers attention, and what decision does it trigger? A dashboard isn't a report — every number earns its place by pointing at an action.
4

The review cadence

A dashboard without a review cadence is a screensaver. The cadence is the discipline.
Weekly is the lesson's recommendation.
Same slot every time — protected.
Owner-led — that's non-negotiable.
The three questions that structure every review
1
Where is each number versus the prior week?
2
Which one is moving in a direction that requires a decision?
3
What are we going to do about it before next week's review?
5

The commitment

A first review on the calendar, an owner, and what gets dropped to make room.
Who keeps the numbers current — distinct from who runs the review.
One shared sheet beats a new piece of software. Name the tool and the update routine.
A new 30-minute ritual needs 30 minutes from somewhere. Name it, or it won't hold.
